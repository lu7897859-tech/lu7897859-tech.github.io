Is your brand already registered in China?

You can look this up yourself, for free, in about ten minutes — in the same public registry everyone else uses. This page shows you how. No signup, nothing to buy.

Why this can happen without you ever going to China

China's trademark office (CNIPA) grants marks on a first-to-file basis. Whoever files first holds the mark — even if they have never used the brand, and even if they never sell anything.

Filing fees are low, and no Chinese address or business entity is required to file. Filings can be made through agents from anywhere. This is sometimes called “trademark squatting.” This page mostly avoids that word, because whether any specific filing was made in bad faith is a legal determination — not something a website can make. Everything below is registry fact.

What the example does show: the same brand string can sit in the registry under multiple unrelated owners at the same time, in different classes. Whether any of it matters to you depends on what you sell and where you are headed — a question for a qualified professional, not a website.

How to check yourself (free, ~10 minutes)

1. TMview — the fastest English entry point

Go to tmdn.org → TMview. Free, English, no login. Search your brand string and filter the office to China (CN). Then read three things per result: owner name, classes, status.

2. Madrid Monitor — if you file internationally

If your brand goes through the Madrid System, check which registrations designate China, and where they stand.

3. CNIPA's own database — the authoritative source

Current status is best confirmed at the source. Chinese UI, occasional captcha. One thing people miss: search the Chinese-character version of your brand, too. Filings often use Chinese characters rather than the Latin string, and a Latin-only search misses them entirely.

Two caveats worth repeating.
Every result you see is a snapshot: a pending application can later be refused, and a registered mark can lapse. And whether any filing is “bad faith” is a legal determination — do not take it from a random website, including this one.
